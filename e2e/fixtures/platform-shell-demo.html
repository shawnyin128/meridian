LongSpec: Long-Context Lossless Speculative Decoding
Wei Yang¹   Ming Chen¹   Harper Liu²   Sofia Ortiz²
¹Institute of Computing Systems   ²Meridian Labs  ·  arXiv:2607.01428v2
Abstract
Speculative decoding reduces the latency of autoregressive LLM inference by letting a lightweight draft model propose multiple tokens that the target model verifies in parallel. Tree-structured drafting generalizes this idea, but prior work consistently reports a knee point beyond which wider draft trees stop paying off. We revisit this conclusion in batched long-context serving and show that the knee point is a property of the serving regime, not of the method. LongSpec achieves up to 2.4× end-to-end speedup on 32k-context workloads.
1  Introduction

Large language models are increasingly served with long contexts and high request concurrency. In such regimes decoding latency is dominated by memory traffic rather than arithmetic, which speculative decoding attacks by trading extra parallel verification for fewer sequential steps.

A widely cited limitation is that the benefit of tree-structured drafts saturates quickly: EAGLE-2 and SpecInfer both report diminishing returns beyond modest tree widths, and this knee point has since been treated as a fixed property of the method.

In batched long-context serving, the verification cost of wide draft trees is amortized across the batch: attention over the shared prefix is computed once and reused by every candidate branch, so wider trees remain net-positive well beyond the previously reported knee point.

We formalize this observation into a cost model (§3), build a drafting and verification scheduler on top of it, and evaluate on production-shaped workloads (§5). Our contributions are threefold: a regime-aware cost model, the LongSpec scheduler, and an empirical characterization of when wide trees pay.

3.2  Amortized verification under shared prefixes

Let B denote batch size and L the shared prefix length. Verification cost per candidate branch decomposes into a prefix term, computed once per batch, and a branch term proportional to tree width. As B grows with prefix reuse enabled, the prefix term is amortized and the effective cost per branch approaches the branch term alone.

6  Conclusion

Knee-point results for tree drafting are properties of the serving regime, not of the method. Batched long-context serving with shared prefixes admits substantially wider trees than single-request analysis suggests.
